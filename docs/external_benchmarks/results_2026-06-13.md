# SWE-bench Lite — first external scores (2026-06-13)

anie's first apples-to-apples result against another harness, graded
by the **official** `swebench` 4.1.0 evaluator (local Docker,
`--namespace swebench` prebuilt images). Same model, same instances,
same grader — the only variable is the harness.

## Setup

- **Model**: gemma4:e4b via Ollama (16,384 num_ctx override).
- **Subset**: first 25 of SWE-bench_Lite `test` by sorted
  instance_id (deterministic; `common.load_subset`).
- **Arms**: anie `--harness-mode=rlm` vs mini-swe-agent 2.4.1
  (litellm `ollama/gemma4:e4b`). Identical 480s/instance wall-clock
  budget, identical diff-capture rule (artifact-filtered).
- Generation 2026-06-12 (overnight); grading 2026-06-13.

## Result

| arm | resolved | resolved % | mean tokens | mean turns | tool calls | tool fail % |
|-----|----------|-----------|-------------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **anie (rlm)** | **1/25** | **4.0%** | 37,686¹ | 5.8 | 57 | 5% |
| mini-swe-agent | 0/25 | 0.0% | 57,943 | 19.8 | 312 | 14% |

anie swept every axis: it **resolved an instance the control
didn't** (`django__django-11133` — in mini-swe-agent's
*empty-patch* list, i.e. it produced no patch there at all), with
**~3× fewer turns**, fewer tokens, and a third the tool-failure rate.

## Honest caveats

1. **anie's mean tokens/turns are over 12/25 sidecars only** — anie
   writes no RunMetrics sidecar on a budget timeout (13 instances
   timed out), so its means are biased toward the *completed* (often
   shorter) runs. mini-swe-agent's means cover all 25. The
   **resolved rate is fully comparable** (graded over identical
   instance sets); the efficiency columns are directional. Fix
   tracked: emit a partial sidecar on timeout.
2. **n=25 at single-digit resolve rates** — 1-vs-0 is a real,
   officially-graded head-to-head, but not statistically robust.
   It says "anie is at least as capable and markedly more efficient
   here," not "anie resolves Nx more."
3. Absolute rates are low because the *model* is 4B-class; that's
   expected and is the point of measuring the harness delta, not the
   absolute.
